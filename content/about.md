# About the series

**Implementation became cheap before alignment did.**

Engineering at AI Speed is a series by Kurt Mitchell about the engineering practices that make accelerated implementation useful: shared definitions, explicit constraints, architecture, and evidence.

AI expands who can implement and how much a team can accomplish. The questions about identity, ownership, failure, and verification still need answers. This series explores how to bring those decisions forward without rebuilding heavyweight bureaucracy.

## Who it is for

Engineers, architects, product owners, analysts, designers, and anyone using AI to turn an idea into software. Each episode starts with a concrete engineering situation and develops a practice readers can apply.

## The first season

A short prologue introduces the argument. Eight focused episodes move from terminology and integration environments to requirements, architectural ownership, executable constraints, and review.

Episodes are released one at a time. [Read the series](index.html#episodes) or [subscribe to the RSS feed](feed.xml).

## About the author

Kurt Mitchell is a software engineer and architect working on enterprise systems and integrations. His writing connects specific engineering problems to the architectural decisions behind them.

His other series, [The Pattern Mirror](https://infinyte.github.io/Pattern-Mirror/), explores connections between infrastructure and application patterns. Engineering at AI Speed has its own focus and identity, with the same emphasis on concrete examples and plain language.

## Sources and corrections

Technical references appear alongside the claims they support. Project-local vocabulary is identified explicitly. Hypothetical examples illustrate a design question and should not be read as reports of observed incidents.

For a correction or a question, [open an issue in the repository](https://github.com/infinyte/Engineering-at-AI-Speed/issues). Include the article title and the passage you are referring to.
